The Sensing, Learning, and Understanding for Robotic Manipulation (SLURM) Lab at the University of Southern California is a Physical AI lab. We develop robots that can perceive, reason about, and skillfully interact with the messy, unstructured physical world. A central theme of our work is scalable data generation for robot learning. We develop methods that use simulation, human videos, and other scalable sources to produce the large and diverse datasets that capable robot policies need. We are especially excited about dexterous hands and humanoids, and about enabling these robots to take full advantage of their own embodiment for richer and more human-like manipulation. Our long-term goal is to build robots that work reliably outside the lab, in homes, hospitals, warehouses, and beyond. Professor Daniel Seita directs the SLURM Lab.

We are actively recruiting students. If you are interested in joining the lab, please see the Getting Involved page.

Why the name SLURM? :-) It's a reference to the cluster management and job scheduling system used for Linux clusters. It is very helpful for many research labs!
